Let $R$ be a ring, and $a, b \in R$. Prove that $-(a+b) = (-a) + (-b)$. Deduce that $(-n)a = -(na)$ equals $n(-a)$ for every nonnegative integer $n$.

>[!answer]-
> Let $R$ be a ring, and $a, b \in R$. From [[Exercise 3.6]], we have $-(a + b) = (-1)(a + b) = (-1) a + (-1) b = -a + (-b)$. Then by induction, 
> $$
> -(na) = -(\underbrace{a + a + \cdots + a}_{n\ \text{times}}) = \underbrace{(-a) + (-a) + \cdots + (-a)}_{n\ \text{times}} = n(-a)
> $$
>
